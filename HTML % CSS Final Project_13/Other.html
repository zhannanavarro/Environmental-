What is the cause Environmental problem?

Climate Change and Fossil FuelsThe widespread burning of fossil fuels—such as coal, oil, and natural gas—is the primary driver of global climate change. When these fuels are combusted for electricity, heating, and transportation, they release massive amounts of greenhouse gases, particularly carbon dioxide, into the atmosphere. This layer of gases acts like a blanket, trapping heat and causing global temperatures to rise. The resulting climate disruption triggers severe consequences, including extreme weather events, rising sea levels, and catastrophic disruptions to global agriculture and water supplies.

Deforestation and Habitat LossDeforestation is rapidly stripping the planet of its vital green canopy, largely driven by agricultural expansion, logging, and urbanization. Forests act as the earth's lungs, absorbing vast quantities of carbon dioxide and releasing oxygen. When millions of hectares of trees are cleared or burned, that stored carbon is released back into the air, accelerating global warming. Furthermore, the destruction of these ecosystems leads to a devastating loss of biodiversity, driving countless plant and animal species toward extinction and disrupting natural ecological balances.

Plastic Pollution and WasteThe modern global economy is heavily reliant on single-use plastics, creating an unprecedented waste crisis that threatens global ecosystems. Because synthetic plastics take hundreds of years to decompose, the vast majority of discarded items end up in landfills or escape into natural environments. Millions of tons of plastic waste enter our oceans every year, breaking down into toxic microplastics. These particles contaminate the marine food web, kill wildlife through ingestion or entanglement, and ultimately pose serious health risks to humans who consume seafood.

Resource Overconsumption Modern consumer culture relies on the rapid extraction and consumption of the earth's finite natural resources. From freshwater and minerals to fertile soil and timber, humans are consuming resources at a rate far faster than the planet can naturally replenish them. This relentless demand fuels destructive mining operations, intensive farming, and industrial manufacturing, all of which deplete local water tables and degrade land. The resulting strain on the environment threatens future resource security and generates massive amounts of industrial waste.

How to prevent this problems?

Module 1 Mitigation: Transitioning to Renewable Energy Frameworks Mitigating global greenhouse gas emissions requires an immediate transition from fossil fuels to clean, renewable energy sources. Implementing utility-scale solar, wind, and hydroelectric power infrastructure eliminates the carbon footprint associated with electricity generation. On a localized level, implementing energy-efficiency measures—such as upgrading to LED lighting, utilizing smart thermostats, and improving building insulation—drastically reduces grid demand. Furthermore, replacing internal combustion engine vehicles with electric vehicles (EVs) and expanding electric public transit grids addresses the primary drivers of transport-related emissions.
